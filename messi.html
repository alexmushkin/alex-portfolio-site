Why Do We Love Sports?

Messi

Last night, I did 25 minutes of research, then spent another 10 signing up for a free trial I will no doubt forget to cancel for a streaming platform I have never heard of. I did this on a Tuesday night for a man I have never met, one I will never meet, and who has never heard of me. I do not know his family or friends. I am not from his country or his continent. Still, I dropped everything I was doing and entered my credit card details to watch him play a game, commentated in a language I do not understand. This man is Lionel Messi, the Argentinian superstar footballer who, on October 6th, played his final game for the national team.

I don’t even watch soccer, but I watched the entire pregame show. Every camera clamored for a look at Messi in the tunnel, through the jerseys of his teammates, during speeches, and around corners. Every third Spanish word was his name, and despite my lack of comprehension, I was certain the topic never shifted. I neglected responsibilities and dinner. A #10 jersey stretched nearly the entire width of the pitch. A chorus of children in the tunnel chanted his name, preceding 85,000 fans who did the same. I told myself watching the kickoff was extremely important, so I kept my eyes glued to the screen as Benin took the first touch.

Every touch from Messi drew the eyes and audible inhales from the crowd. Every saved shot and deflection delayed the celebration. Like everyone in Estadio Mâs Monumental, I felt that Messi’s farewell necessitated one final shot in the back of the net for his country. So I watched every minute. In the 71st, Messi lined up to take a penalty. I found myself almost unable to look. When the ball sailed into the top right of the goal off his left foot, I felt joy but mostly relief. I kept watching until the final whistle blew, just in case there was a moment that would be talked about or replayed for years. I watched as a Beninese player chased Messi down with a phone in hand, hoping to capture a memento from the night they shared the pitch, proof to quiet the doubts of his future grandchildren.

Messi’s stature nearly got me to believe the internet today when it told me 3.5 billion people watched this match. He has been bringing his entire country and more together for 21 years. Last night, from the comfort of his home on the pitch, he made me care. He gifted anxiety, hope, passion, and the childlike desire for a fairytale ending to a stranger 5,196 miles away. These games burden us with interest and bless us with belonging under the guise of entertainment. These players let us believe in a dream, even if it will never be our own, it is never theirs alone.
